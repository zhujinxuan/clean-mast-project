# Research: rep-year calibration benchmarking; short-observation policy (ticket 18)

Date: 2026-09-02. Question: (1) can/should rep-year calibration methods be benchmarked, and with which exam design; (2) what does practice do when the on-site (overlap) record is ≤ 1 year.

## Q1 — Measurement-duration and seasonal-coverage rules

**12 complete consecutive months is the universal normative minimum; the operative requirement is seasonal coverage, enforced via completeness gates and (for sub-year data) uncertainty inflation, not refusal.**

- **MEASNET v3 (2022) §7.2** (official PDF, p.14): "The measurement period shall cover at least 12 complete and consecutive months for at least one mast at the site, in order to appropriately assess seasonal variations." The measurement is **incomplete** (a *deviation* to be stated and carried into the uncertainty assessment, §5) if: no mast covers ≥ 12 consecutive months; filtered-data availability (primary + backup sensor) < **90%** in 12 consecutive months; or MCP-filled data availability < **95%** in 12 consecutive months. Wind direction §7.3: < 95% filled availability ⇒ incomplete. https://measnet.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ce599b8a-639f-4b48-992e-a099e1b14956.pdf
- **MEASNET §8.3.2–8.3.4**: the concurrent (correlation) period "should include at least one year, especially if the extrapolation cannot be performed on a time series basis and if the differences between the short-term and reference data are large"; minimum recommended correlation **R = 0.8** (R² = 0.64); in-sample MBE/MAE/RMSE against the training data "must be checked and documented" for all MCP methods; reference data = "database of at least 10 years", "a period of ten years is generally considered as a reasonable compromise". §8.5: uncertainty components include "Length of input time series / Overlapping period / Correlation between site and reference data / … / Inter-annual variability", and: "**If the period of the site measurement is less than one year, the uncertainty of the extrapolation is significantly increased**" (especially for large height differences, via seasonal stratification). For long-term scaling it must be shown that "the extrapolation procedure was generally validated also for short periods". And: "The quality of the applied MCP extrapolation and the validity of the MCP result **shall** be tested by performing a self-consistency test." (same PDF)
- **FGW TR6** (current: Rev 12, Nov 2023; wind-fgw.de "Guideline Work" page): the German wind-potential/yield guideline. Basse et al. (2021, WES 6:1473, https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/6/1473/2021/): "an amount of 12 months of measurement is recommended or even a mandatory minimum duration due to technical guidelines and standards such as FGW e.V. (2020), IEC (2017), or MEASNET (2016)"; availability demand > 80% (FGW) per Jonietz Alvarez et al. (2024, WES 9:2217, as verified in the sibling note `mcp-benchmarking.md`).
- **IEC 61400-15-1:2025** (site suitability input conditions; verified from FDIS 88/1041/FDIS, https://www.aresca.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/88_1041e_FDIS.pdf): for model-derived conditions, "Ideally, at least one year of on-site measurements shall be required to check the seasonal representativeness of global or mesoscale models" (§5.2.2.4); for wind shear, "When using non-integer year(s) of wind shear data it is key to ensure data applied is seasonally unbiased" (§5.5.1); for the wind-speed distribution: "If the data is not representative, **long-term adjustment shall be carried out** by using either long-term adjustment factor(s), a Measure Correlate and Predict (MCP) method … or a more advanced method"; temperature and air density: "A long-term adjustment should be performed if necessary."
- **IEC 61400-15-2:2022** (site-specific conditions assessment / energy yield): paywalled; existence/scope per IEC webstore and the DTU-authored weMEP protocol description (https://wemep.readthedocs.io/en/latest/windconditions/windconditions.html — 15-2 covers the AEP/P50/σ reporting framework). Its campaign posture is known here only via the Basse quote above ("IEC (2017)") and MEASNET alignment — not independently verified.
- **IEC 61400-12-1:2017** is *power performance testing*, not WRA: no calendar minimum; database completeness is defined by wind-speed-bin coverage, not duration (verified in the CSA sample: Annex H normatively modifies §8.5 for small turbines to "each wind speed bin … minimum of 10 min of sampled data; the total database contains at least 60 h"). Seasonal mismatch enters as an uncertainty component ("uncertainty due to SC and PC recorded in different seasons", MEASNET 2024 proposal for 12-1 ed.3, https://measnet.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/8fea5746-709e-4563-8b7a-d5ecf58e7581.pdf, §4.5.13). Not a source for rep-year policy.
- **China, GB/T 18709-2002** (measurement): continuous on-site observation **≥ 1 full year** ("数据采集时间不应少于1 a" — 王哲 2016, 内蒙古电力技术 34(1):37–40, doi:10.3969/j.issn.1008-6218.2016.01.002, https://html.rhhz.net/nmgdljs/20160102.htm; same article: practice prefers 2–3 years, and attributes a real underperforming Inner-Mongolia farm partly to a 1-year-only campaign). Official record (status: current): https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/std/newGbInfo?hcno=805F8F66F978CBD8F9504B92316883C8. Standard-text reproductions add ≥ 98% collection completeness (as reproduced, e.g. https://www.bzchaxun.com/view/6242125001000000.html).
- **China, GB/T 18710-2002** (assessment; full text at https://notes.hehugo.com/references/技术规范/风电/风电场风能资源评估方法（GB-T 18710-2002）.html, official record std.samr.gov.cn id=71F772D75E3ED3A7E05397BE0A0AB82A): §5.2.1 data validation must yield "**at least one continuous complete year**" of hourly site data; §5.2.4 **有效数据完整率 ≥ 90%** (explicit formula: (应测−缺测−无效)/应测); §4.1.2 the nearby long-term station must supply "representative consecutive **30 years**" of annual/monthly means plus concurrent hourly data; §5.3 **数据订正** (informative Annex A) corrects site data against that station into "代表年" — **representative-year** hourly wind data at measurement height; §5.3.2 station must show good concurrent correlation, ≥ 30-year records, similar terrain, proximity; §6.1.3 requires 20–30-year inter-annual variation plots. This is the Chinese rep-year precedent: correct the site record to a representative year anchored on a 30-year station.
- **China, NB/T 31147-2018** (风电场工程风能资源测量与评估技术规范): ≥ 1 full year of measurement; long-series benchmark ideally 30 years; if the long-term annual mean deviates > ~2% from the benchmark period, site data are corrected to the representative annual level per GB/T 18710 (as reproduced from the standard text, https://max.book118.com/html/2024/0513/7024026042006106.shtm). **NB/T 31105-2025** (陆上风电场工程可行性研究报告编制规程): mast data completeness/effectiveness ≥ 90%; representative-year data corrected with multi-source fusion; long-term reference series ≥ 20 years (as reproduced, https://www.ebzbz.com/hangyebiaozhun/nengyuan/nb-t-31105-2025.html).

## Q2 — MCP / long-term-correction skill vs overlap length (sub-year focus)

- **Uncertainty grows rapidly below one year.** Olsen, Lybech Thøgersen, Bechmann & Svensson (2021), "The influence of seasonal biases on long-term correction uncertainty", DTU Wind Energy E-0225 (RECAST; 9 sites N. Europe + South Africa, WRF reference; pseudo short campaigns carved from long records): "Short-term measurement campaigns are currently unfeasible because the mean wind speed uncertainty grows rapidly with a shortening of the measurement time-window below one year"; even with *full knowledge* of seasonal reference-data biases, "for very-short-term (1–2 months) campaigns the uncertainties remain about **three times larger** than a full-year campaign"; with such knowledge 3–9-month campaigns could gain "upwards of 50%" uncertainty reduction. https://orbit.dtu.dk/en/publications/1d6f7ee6-e0bb-4807-a03d-cf884ab1a7b0
- **But sub-year correction usually still beats the reference alone — with a correlation gate.** Sheridan et al. (2025, WES 10:1451, 66 US sites, 3.5–23-year records used as validation truth; 75% monthly recovery required for inclusion): "correction with even 1 month of observations provides significant improvement over the baseline ERA5 … within 0.22 m s⁻¹ and 4 percentage points of … the standard 12 months"; **however** "when the shortest observational periods (1 to 2 months) … are not well correlated with the overlapping ERA5 reference, the resultant long-term wind speed errors are **worse** than those produced using ERA5 without correction"; worst-correlation capacity-factor relative errors at 1/3/6 months ≈ **47%/26%/16%**; summer months (esp. Jul–Aug) worst; regression trees degrade vs MLR/ARS below 6 months. https://wes.copernicus.org/articles/10/1451/2025/
- **~3 months is workable with care; season and method decide the sign of the bias.** As compiled by Sheridan (2025, intro): 3-month corrections gave wind-speed errors within ~4% (Taylor et al. 2004, via Carta 2013), 4.8% (Weekes & Tomlin 2014), and ~4% (Basse et al. 2021); Derrick (1992, via Rogers 2005) found 8 months needed to minimize uncertainty; Weekes & Tomlin: smallest errors for autumn/early-spring training. Basse et al. (2021, full text this session): with 90-day sliding windows (122 per site, 18 German sites, 6 reanalyses), VR overestimates for summer training / underestimates for winter while LR-residuals mostly shows the **opposite sign**; all six reanalyses overpronounced the annual cycle; the site–reference correlation has a seasonal cycle (drops in summer; some EMD pairs < 0.5); sectorwise (direction-binned) fits "generally yielded slightly worse results" at 3 months; mean-speed error is weighted ~6× variance error for energy. Bias theory (their Eq. 13): sub-year bias = representativeness of the measurement period − β₁-weighted representativeness of the reference.
- **Longer campaigns keep helping past 1 year.** Miguel et al. (2019, Energies 12:3606, Brazil): WRA uncertainty falls 18/29/35/40% when 1/2/3/4 years of measurement are added (as reported by Sheridan 2025). Abascal Mendez et al. (2026, Eng. Proc. 11(2):23, https://www.mdpi.com/2411-5134/11/2/23) quantify uncertainty vs campaign length for linear vs ML MCP with rolling one-month start dates.
- **Analytic duration models in tooling.** windPRO MCP manual §7.2: uncorrected local-data uncertainty = annual wind-speed variability / √(period length in years) — "statistical independence is not always given" (NAO/ENSO); §7.3.1 correlation "should preferably be better than 0.7"; §7.5 the Klintø (2015) LTC uncertainty model Unc = √[(A·|1−WI|)² + (B·RC)² + (D·V)²]·YE·CF turns the **wind index WI of the concurrent period** (i.e., how unrepresentative the measured year is) and the number of concurrent years into production uncertainty. https://help.emd.dk/knowledgebase/content/ReferenceManual/MCP.pdf
- **Caveat on any within-record exam:** year-to-year persistence makes fold-level errors optimistic (Bodini et al. 2016, WES 1:115); inter-annual wind-speed variability is 4–10% and energy 6–30% (survey in Basse et al. 2021 intro).

## Q3 — Documented validation designs (is a calibration exam a thing?)

**Yes — the ticket's candidate exam is the canonical validation design of the field: carve pseudo-campaign windows out of a long record, apply the correction, score against that record's known long-term statistics.**

- Basse et al. (2021): 122 sliding 90-day windows (3-day step, wrap-around "so that all seasons are considered equally") per site × 6 references; corrected series scored against the measured year (their explicit benchmark); error scores = bias in annual mean, variance, theoretical AEP.
- Sheridan et al. (2025): months-long windows (1–12 months) at 66 sites; model predicts "the full observation period"; performance vs the site's multi-year record; 75%-monthly-recovery inclusion rule.
- Weekes & Tomlin (2014a, Renewable Energy 63:162; manuscript https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/id/eprint/78143/): 3-month-trained MCP replicated by sliding a 1-year window in 1-month steps over 11-year records (test periods integer years to avoid seasonal leakage) — the "hold-out years" variant.
- Olsen et al. (2021, RECAST): 9 long records + WRF reference; sub-year observation windows swept to produce uncertainty-vs-duration curves, including a counterfactual "if seasonal reference bias were known" arm.
- Miguel et al. (2019) and Abascal Mendez et al. (2026): campaign-length sweeps on multi-year masts.
- **Tool-shipped exams:** Windographer "Test MCP Uncertainty" — bootstrap: "many iterations of the MCP process, each time drawing a different random sample of the available concurrent data, training … creating the extended dataset", recording MoMM wind speed / vector-mean direction / MoMM temperature; example 2500 iterations → MoMM σ = 0.0546 m/s; "convergence usually occurs within a few hundred iterations" (https://support.ul-renewables.com/windographer/dlg-revise-LTA-test-MCP-uncertainty.htm); plus "Test Algorithm Performance" cross-validation across the 8 algorithms (Correlate Speed tab). windPRO "slicing tests" (manual ch. 8): "the concurrent dataset is divided into two even sized groups. One … ('training') while the other … tests the quality of the transfer function", with explicit anti-leakage warnings ("Examples for biased slicing periods are day versus night or summer versus winter"; the "Second half" option is flagged as seasonally biased for 1-year overlaps), scored by MBE/MAE/RMSE (monthly RMSE "of higher value"), correlation, and a two-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test; "Entire concurrent period" is labeled a self-test; results compared across all MCP models in one table.
- **Normative anchor:** MEASNET v3 §8.5 *requires* a "self-consistency test" of MCP quality/validity; §8.3.2 mandates documented MBE/MAE/RMSE on the training data; MEASNET site-assessment procedure institutionalizes mutual round robins (measnet.com v2 announcement, http://www.measnet.com/2016/05/new-version-of-the-measnet-site-assessment-procedure-evaluation-of-site-specific-wind-conditions-has-been-released/; practice example: 2018 German round robin, 24/29 consultants linear, via Basse et al. 2021).
- No fully *synthetic* (simulated-truth) benchmark found in this corpus; every documented exam uses real long records as truth.

## Q4 — Industry tool behavior for < 1-year on-site records

- **Windographer (UL)** — no hard refusal; guards instead: (i) "Concurrent period limitation … if the datasets overlap by 16 months, you may wish to use only 12 months … so that the concurrent data, and the regressions that result, are not seasonally biased" (https://support.ul-renewables.com/windographer/dlg-revise-LTA.htm); (ii) "Windographer requires at least 24 hours of data in each subdivision, with an absolute minimum of 8 data points. Divisions falling below the required number use the overall regression" (https://support.ul-renewables.com/windographer/dlg-revise-LTA-correlate-speed.htm); (iii) the cross-validation and bootstrap exams above. So: sub-year allowed, seasonal-bias surfaced to the analyst, no uncertainty multiplier enforced.
- **windPRO (EMD)** — no hard minimum; guidance-level rules: "Keep the local data to an integer number of years in order to avoid seasonal bias"; "Typically, 90% data recovery should be aimed for … if data is missing systematically e.g. due to icing in winter, the result might be biased even when the recovery rate might have reached 90%"; correlation "preferably better than 0.7"; Klintø uncertainty (record length and wind-index representativeness enter analytically); slicing tests carry the seasonal-split warnings (manual ch. 7–8, URL above). Note the RECAST authors are EMD staff — the "sub-year currently unfeasible" finding is the tool vendor's own research position for *bankable* use.
- **OpenOA (NREL/NatLabRockies)** — no pre-construction MCP module; the operational `MonteCarloAEP` regresses plant energy on reanalysis at monthly resolution ("At monthly time resolution only linear regression is allowed because of the reduced number of data points"), normalizes via a **windiness correction** whose window is Monte-Carlo sampled in **[10, 20] years**, raises a hard `ValueError` only when the *reanalysis* record is too short for that window, quantifies IAV (std/mean of annual gross energy), and documents the on-site period of record as "shorter … (e.g., 1–3 years)" with no calendar minimum enforced (source read: https://raw.githubusercontent.com/NREL/OpenOA/main/openoa/analysis/aep.py).

## Verdict

**(a) Documented minimum-duration / seasonal-coverage rules.** Twelve complete consecutive months of on-site data is the normative minimum across MEASNET (shall; v3 §7.2), FGW TR6 (mandatory per Basse 2021) and China (GB/T 18709-2002 ≥ 1 year; GB/T 18710-2002 "at least one continuous complete year", ≥ 90% effective completeness; NB/T 31147-2018 ≥ 1 year); IEC 15-1 phrases it as seasonal representativeness ("ideally at least one year") plus a *shall*-level duty to long-term-adjust non-representative data. Availability gates: 90% (MEASNET filtered; GB/T 18710 effective), 95% (MEASNET MCP-filled), 80% (FGW). No standard *forbids* sub-year data — MEASNET's instrument is the declared **deviation with significantly increased uncertainty**, and China's GB/T 18710 §5.3 is itself a rep-year correction procedure (site data corrected against a 30-year station into 代表年 data). Recommended reference length: ~10 years (MEASNET) / 20–30 years (NB/T 31105; GB/T 18710 §4.1.2); correlation floor R ≥ 0.8 recommended (MEASNET), r > 0.7 preferred (windPRO).

**(b) Is calibration-method benchmarking done in practice, and with which exam?** Yes. The documented exam is exactly the ticket's candidate: contiguous pseudo-campaign windows held out from long records, corrected, and scored against the record's known long-term statistics — replicated across many windows (seasonal spread) and sites/references (Basse 2021; Sheridan 2025; Weekes & Tomlin 2014; Miguel 2019; Abascal Mendez 2026; RECAST 2021). A second family — within-record train/test *slicing* — ships inside the commercial tools (Windographer cross-validation + bootstrap "Test MCP Uncertainty"; windPRO slicing tests with MBE/MAE/RMSE/correlation/KS and explicit summer-vs-winter leakage warnings), and MEASNET mandates a self-consistency test. No synthetic-truth benchmark found.

**(c) Evidence on MCP skill vs overlap length.** Error decreases monotonically with overlap; the curve steepens below ~9–12 months and falls off a cliff below ~3: 3-month corrections land within ~4–4.8% mean wind speed with season/method care; 1 month still beats an ERA5-only baseline *on average*, but 1–2-month windows with weak correlation can be **worse than no correction** (Sheridan 2025); even with perfect seasonal-bias knowledge, 1–2-month campaigns retain ~3× full-year uncertainty (RECAST 2021). Season of the overlap matters (avoid Jul–Aug / summer; VR and LR carry opposite-sign seasonal biases; correlation itself dips in summer), and record length/seasonal coverage dominates method choice (Bass 2000 via Basse 2021; Carta 2013; 2018 round robin).

**(d) v1 recommendation.**
1. **Do not build a calibration-method benchmark in v1.** v1 ships one calibration method (Julia-semantics per-sector slopes), so a selection benchmark has nothing to select; and the literature says method ranking is weakly separated (Δ ≈ 0.05 m/s across regression variants per the sibling note) while overlap length and seasonal coverage dominate error — a v1 exam would mostly re-measure seasonal bias, not method quality. Keep the registry additive (`repyear.*`): when the 行标 two-stage procedure (or any second method) is registered, benchmark *then*, reusing the documented design — hold-out contiguous windows from the long meso record as pseudo-campaigns, score calibrated long-term statistics against the meso record's actual climatology, many windows for seasonal spread, with anti-leakage splitting rules copied from windPRO's warnings (never train summer/test winter).
2. **Cheap v1 substitute:** log the MEASNET §8.3.2 in-sample diagnostics (MBE/MAE/RMSE of the calibration fit) as a report quantity — no gate.
3. **Short-mast policy (rep-year calibration when the mast's native overlap with the reference is < 12 months): tiered correct-with-uncertainty, refuse only at the bottom.**
   - ≥ 12 complete months: calibrate normally.
   - 6–11 complete months: **correct + flag** (MEASNET deviation route; uncertainty materially increased; Sheridan: 6-month worst-case CF error ≈ 16%).
   - 3–5 months: **correct only if the correlation gate passes** (r ≥ 0.7 at the calibration timescale, matching windPRO's floor; MEASNET recommends R ≥ 0.8), else skip; always flag low-confidence; prefer overlaps avoiding Jul–Aug.
   - < 3 months: **refuse** — emit the skip flag (existing `rep-year-skipped` token semantics), leave the series uncalibrated. Grounds: RECAST ~3× uncertainty; Sheridan worse-than-uncorrected risk for 1–2-month, weakly correlated windows.
   - Every tier: require unbiased monthly coverage inside the used overlap (no single-season fits), mirroring windPRO's "integer number of years" rule and GB/T 18710's 90% effective completeness. Refusal is reserved for the regime where the literature shows correction can *harm*; everything else is flagged correction, never silent.

Feeds ticket 12 Q4: calibration methods do **not** benchmark in v1 (exam design is documented and ready for the registry's second method); short-mast policy = tiered flag/correct with a 3-month refusal floor and an r ≥ 0.7 gate.
