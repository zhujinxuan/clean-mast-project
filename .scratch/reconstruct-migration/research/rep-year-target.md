# Research: representative-year target — actual year vs climatology mean (ticket 17)

Date: 2026-09-02. Question: in MCP / long-term (representative-year) correction, should the calibration target be an **actual year** selected from the reference record (real year whose mean is within a threshold of the long-term mean — Julia CleanMast status quo) or the **climatology mean** itself (Chinese 行标 procedure per ticket 12)? Sub-questions: standards guidance, industry tool behavior, published evidence on consequences, and whether the ~2% threshold + latest-qualifying-year rule is documented anywhere.

**Verification note.** Read in full from publisher/official sources: MEASNET "Evaluation of Site-Specific Wind Conditions" V3 (Sep 2022) §8 + glossary; IEC 61400-15-1 FDIS (88/1041/FDIS, 2024) §5.2.1; GB/T 18710-2002 full text (verbatim reproduction at notes.hehugo.com, cross-checked against the official std.samr.gov.cn record); FGW TR6 Rev.12 official preview (front matter, definitions, ToC only — §4 body is paywalled); NREL/TP-5000-64735 (§3.3.1, §4). ## Q1 — Standards & industry guidance: what target does each prescribe?

**Every international standard targets the long-term (climatology) wind conditions over a ≥10-year reference record. None prescribes selecting an actual calendar year as the target.**

- **IEC 61400-15-1:2025** (read from FDIS 88/1041/FDIS, §5.2.1.1–5.2.1.2): "The wind speed distribution shall be representative of the operational lifetime of the wind turbine. If the data is not representative, long-term adjustment **shall** be carried out by using either long-term adjustment factor(s), a Measure Correlate and Predict (MCP) method … or a more advanced method." The prescribed target is a lifetime-representative long-term distribution; no representative-year selection exists anywhere in the standard (reporting Annex B.2.7 asks for "Long-term adjusted wind data"). https://www.aresca.us/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/88_1041e_FDIS.pdf
- **MEASNET, Evaluation of Site-Specific Wind Conditions V3 (2022)**, §8.3: long-term extrapolation projects measured data "to long-term wind conditions which are considered to be representative for typical mean wind conditions and the best estimation for the future wind conditions" (§8.3.1). Two method families (§8.3.2): (i) **MCP** — fit relations on the concurrent period, then "apply the determined relations on the long-term reference data to extrapolate the short-term data to the long-term period" (sectoral regression MCP, matrix MCP, others); (ii) **long-term scaling** — wind/energy index or distribution scaling, applied "to extrapolate them to a long-term period". Requirements: concurrent period ≥ 12 complete consecutive months; correlation period "should include at least one year" for seasonal independence; recommended minimum correlation R = 0.8; reference data = "database of at least 10 years (including the measurement period)" (glossary). In both families the target is the long-term period or its distribution — never a selected actual year. https://measnet.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ce599b8a-639f-4b48-992e-a099e1b14956.pdf
- **FGW TR6 Rev.12 (2023)** structures long-term correction as: Kurzzeitdaten / Langzeitdaten-Bezugsdaten / Abgleichszeitraum (comparison period) / **Bezugszeitraum** (reference period) / Verfahren (§4.1–4.5 of ToC; official preview, body paywalled). Its definitions fix the semantics: the "Anwendungsmodus" transfers wind conditions either "als Statistik (Weibull-Verteilung oder Häufigkeitstabelle) … oder zeitschrittweise" (statistics mode or time-series mode), and reanalysis data are valued because their "große zeitliche Konsistenz … für die Zwecke des Langzeitabgleichs von großem Vorteil ist". Non-compliance handling: MUSS = non-conforming report; a SOLL deviation "muss nachvollziehbar begründet und dokumentiert werden sowie zwingend in der Unsicherheiten-Betrachtung berücksichtigt werden" (deviation must be justified and reflected in the uncertainty budget). https://wind-fgw.de/wp-content/uploads/2024/03/FGW_Teil6_Rev12_20231128_preview_d.pdf . German practice on reference-period length: the ≥30-year requirement was dropped by the Windgutachterbeirat around 2010; 10–15 years often suffice for a stable mean inland, longer along the North Sea coast (Wind-Lexikon "Bezugszeitraum", anemos-jacob — **secondary**, consistent with MEASNET's ≥10-year minimum). https://www.wind-lexikon.de/cms/lexikon/86-lexikon-b/190-bezugszeitraum.html
- **IEA Wind**: the published Recommended-Practice list (RP 01–20) contains **no** RP on resource assessment / MCP / long-term correction (closest: RP 11 anemometry, RP 15 remote sensing). https://iea-wind.org/recommended-pratices/ . The companion overview, NREL/TP-5000-64735 (Clifton, Smith & Fields 2016), states MCP "is a complex process that is **not clearly defined in an existing recommended practice or standard** … Typically, each IE [independent engineer] has their own approach", describes MCP as using references "typically from 10 years–30 years" to "Correct site data to long-term record", and treats inter-annual variability as a separate uncertainty category (§3.3.1, Table 4; uncertainty categories "Historic wind resource" and "Project lifetime variability", §4.2.5–4.2.6, Table 11). https://www.nrel.gov/docs/fy16osti/64735.pdf
- **DNV** (legacy Risø lineage): WindFarmer's Long Term Adjustment derives "target long-term hub-height wind speeds" by regression correlation, synthetic-site-data creation, splicing, and optionally averaging predicted long-term winds across several references — again a long-term mean target, no year selection. https://mysoftware.dnv.com/download/public/renewables/windfarmer/manuals/latest/UserGuide/Wind/Reconstruction/LongTermAdjustment.html . The Danish/German wind-index (energy-index) method — the legacy DNV/Risø-world scaling approach — is codified as one of MEASNET's two long-term scaling families (MEASNET V3 §8.3.2, above).

**The Chinese lineage is the only one found that names a 代表年 (representative year) — but its correction anchor is still the multi-year mean, not a selected year:**

- **GB/T 18710-2002 «风电场风能资源评估方法»** §5.3.1: correction turns verified mast data into "一套反映风场长期平均水平的代表性数据，即风场测风高度上**代表年**的逐小时风速风向数据" — a representative *hourly dataset at measurement height reflecting the long-term average level*. The method (§5.3.4 → Appendix A, informative) is exactly a **16-direction-sector additive-delta correction anchored on the multi-year mean**: (1) build 16 mast↔station wind-speed correlation curves, one per direction quadrant; (2) on each curve mark the station's **多年年平均风速 (multi-year annual mean)** and the station's annual mean for the **mast-concurrent year**; read off the two corresponding mast speeds and take their algebraic difference (16 differences); (3) add each sector's difference to every mast speed in that sector. There is **no year-selection step and no numeric threshold**; the target is the multi-year (climatology) mean; the mast year's own hourly/temporal structure is retained. Preconditions for the long-term station (§5.3.2): good concurrent correlation, ≥30 years of standardized records, similar terrain, close to the site; data to collect include "有代表性的连续 30 年的逐年平均风速" (§4.1.2). Full text: https://notes.hehugo.com/references/技术规范/风电/风电场风能资源评估方法（GB-T 18710-2002）.html (verbatim reproduction; official record https://std.samr.gov.cn/gb/search/gbDetailed?id=71F772D75E3ED3A7E05397BE0A0AB82A). The in-revision GB/T 18710 project adds offshore assessment, ≥20-year station records, mesoscale-data correlation correction with differentiated treatment of poorly-correlated sectors (std.samr.gov.cn project page, https://std.samr.gov.cn/gb/search/gbDetailed?id=413EE73AE912B180E06397BE0A0A1047) — i.e., the Chinese lineage is migrating toward reanalysis-based MCP, not toward year selection.
- **NB/T 31147-2018 «风电场工程风能资源测量与评估技术规范»** (NEA industry standard, onshore; drafted by 水电水利规划设计总院) §5.2 (data inspection/processing), per complete-text copies: choose a long-term reference — preferably national meteorological stations — with a base period "约 30 年" containing the measurement period; **"if the annual mean wind speed deviation exceeds 2%, correct the mast data to representative-year wind speed per GB/T 18710"**; regional reanalysis may be used as additional verification with monthly-mean correlation to the mast not below 0.8. So the **~2% annual-mean deviation gate exists here as a trigger for representative-year correction** — the closest documented analog of CleanMast's need-check. Copies: https://max.book118.com/html/2024/0513/7024026042006106.shtm ; record: https://www.ndls.org.cn/standard/detail/1bb8d9cd52998dbb870f56566c68f820 (full text paywalled — clause wording cited from the indexed document copies, not the official print).
- On ticket 12's 行标 two-stage description ("global offset to climatology first, then per-16-sector linear fit binned by **mast** direction"): the per-16-sector additive correction and the climatology target are verbatim GB/T 18710 Appendix A; the explicit **global-offset-first** step and OLS-with-slope fitting are not found in the standard text I could read — [INFERENCE] that composition is design-institute practice layered on 18710's Appendix A, not standard-mandated mechanics.

## Q2 — Industry tools: actual representative year vs regress/scale toward the long-term mean

**No surveyed tool selects an actual representative year. All produce either a synthesized long-term series over the full reference record or a mean-rescaled measured series.**

- **Windographer (UL)** — Long Term Adjustments window (successor of the MCP module): fit one of LLS/TLS/VR/BSR(WBL)/SpeedSort/VS/MTS on the overlap, then two completion paths: **Extend** the onsite dataset over the full reference POR (e.g. 25 years, hourly, single column) or **Scale** the onsite dataset to align with the extended dataset's long-term mean — the scaled result keeps the short POR (e.g. 1 year), 10-minute resolution and all height levels, "use scaling for time-series energy modeling (retains turbulence, shear, higher resolution); use extending for distribution-focused needs". Both target the long-term statistics of the reference record. https://support.ul-renewables.com/windographer/dlg-revise-LTA.htm ; https://support.ul-renewables.com/windographer/dlg-revise-LTA-synthesize-data.htm ; https://support.ul-renewables.com/windographer/dlg-revise-LTA-correlate-speed.htm
- **WindPRO (EMD) MCP toolbox**: long-term reference may be a time series, a Weibull distribution, or a frequency table — i.e., the target can be a pure long-term climatology with no calendar structure at all; sector-wise transfer functions (direction conditioning, ~1° veer bins, ~30° windows), residual modeling ("without this residual-model option, up to ~10% energy loss can occur"), Monte-Carlo/bootstrap generation of the long-term corrected distribution, plus a Wind Index method recommended when reference data are sparse/weakly correlated (e.g. 6-hourly NCEP/NCAR). https://help.emd.dk/knowledgebase/content/ReferenceManual/MCP.pdf ; https://help.emd.dk/knowledgebase/content/WindPRO2.8/11-UK_WindPRO2.8_MCP.pdf ; https://help.emd.dk/mediawiki/images/8/8c/MCP_Methods.pdf
- **WAsP / DTU ecosystem**: WAsP best practices — "Use an integer number of whole years when calculating the OWC [observed wind climate]" and "Check within context of long-term wind climate (MCP)" (https://www.wasp.dk/-/media/Sites/WASP/Support/FAQ/3-04-WAsP-Best-Practices.ashx?la=da). DTU's open-source **windkit.ltc** implements the WAsP-world MCP as `LinRegMCP` / `VarRatMCP`: sectorwise (default 12 sectors; quantile-based sector widths; ws_cutoff 3 m/s) fit(ref, target) → predict the long-term series (https://docs.wasp.dk/windkit/latest/api_reference/index.html ; https://docs.wasp.dk/windkit/v0.8.0/io/ltc_autogen/windkit.ltc.mcp.LinRegMCP.html).
- **OpenOA (NREL)**: operational (not pre-construction) — `MonteCarloAEP` regresses gross energy on reanalysis wind speed and applies the sampled slope/intercept "to long-term wind speeds (10–20 year horizon)" to produce a long-term AEP distribution; the repository has no MCP/representative-year module. https://openoa.readthedocs.io/en/latest/examples/02a_plant_aep_analysis.html ; https://github.com/NREL/OpenOA
- **brightwind**: correlation classes (OrdinaryLeastSquares, OrthogonalLeastSquares, MultipleLinearRegression, SimpleSpeedRatio, SpeedSort, SVR); canonical workflow: correlate on the overlap → predict the long-term target series → take `momm(data, date_from, date_to)` (mean of monthly means over the chosen long-term window) as the LT target → `scale_wind_speed()` rescales the measured series to that long-term mean. Pure climatology-mean targeting with an explicit long-term-window choice. https://brightwind-dev.github.io/brightwind-docs/tutorials/becoming_a_correlation_wizard.html ; https://brightwind-dev.github.io/brightwind-docs/generated/brightwind.analyse.analyse.momm.html

## Q3 — Published evidence on consequences of the choice

**No published study directly benchmarks actual-year-selected targets against climatology-mean targets within one MCP framework** (negative finding — nothing in the MCP benchmarking literature cited here or in `research/mcp-benchmarking.md` varies the *anchor* while holding the fit fixed). The evidence bearing on the two arguments:

- **Single-year structure can be atypical in exactly the ways the correction does not fix (normative):** MEASNET V3 §8.3.2, wind-index/distribution-scaling methods: "The basis for scaling is usually the wind distribution measured during the short-term period at the site; consequently, the influences of varying wind direction and wind speed shape parameters are not handled. Hence for this method it must be verified, that the wind direction distribution and the Weibull shape parameters during the short-term measurement are not atypical for long-term conditions." This is a documented statement that a single measured year's direction distribution and Weibull shape may deviate from long-term values — and any correction anchored on that year (mean-scaled *or* rep-year-shifted, since both keep the measured year's structure) inherits that deviation. [INFERENCE] The 2% mean gate bounds only the mean-wind-speed part of the year effect, not the direction/shape part.
- **Magnitude of single-year selection noise:** inter-annual variability of wind speed is ~4–10% (6–30% in energy) and year-to-year persistence widens true assessment uncertainty ~3× versus independence assumptions (Bodini et al. 2016, WES 1:115, doi:10.5194/wes-1-115-2016; as compiled in `research/mcp-benchmarking.md`); the mean measurement-year effect is ≈6.5% of the mean (WES 11:661, 2026, doi:10.5194/wes-11-661-2026). So any *actual* year carries a random year-effect of this order; requiring its annual mean to sit within 2% of climatology bounds the residual mean bias but leaves the sector/distribution structure of that particular year.
- **Mean bias dominates energy error, and distribution fidelity is the discriminating metric:** via error propagation on $E[u^3]$, the relative error in the mean is weighted ~6× the relative error in variance (Basse et al. 2021, WES 6:1473, doi:10.5194/wes-6-1473-2021); MCP methods differ ~0.2 m/s / 5% in the mean but ~14% in power density (Weekes & Tomlin 2014a). This cuts both ways: exact mean placement (climatology anchoring) removes a first-order bias term, while preserving a real year's distribution shape avoids the synthetic-construct problem of mean-mapping — but note the two semantics do not differ here: *both* keep the measured mast year as the time-series substrate.
- **Representative one-year constructs are an active research object, and simple selection is the weak baseline:** Lázaro, Melero & Arregui (2025), "Wind Reference Year: A New Approach", Applied Sciences 15(24):13147, doi:10.3390/app152413147: builds a representative 8,760-hour year from 15 years of ERA5 via Gaussian-Mixture-Copula synthesis + regression MCP site adaptation + Hungarian-algorithm temporal reordering (explicitly to preserve wind persistence), validated against 15 years of measurements and benchmarked against "a heuristic [month-]selection method and the Windographer tool", with the synthesized WRY outperforming both. Implication: a one-year construct (whether TMY-style selected or scaled) approximates the long-term statistics with measurable, quantifiable error; the field's response is synthesis toward the long-term distribution, not better year selection. (Content verified via doi.org and repository-record summaries; full text inaccessible.)
- **Practical convergence argument [INFERENCE from the above]:** in CleanMast's algebra the corrected series is always `mast(t) + Δsector × slope`, i.e. the mast year's structure with a shifted mean. Actual-year anchoring only changes *where the mean lands* — at a real year that is within 2% of climatology — versus climatology anchoring which lands exactly on the multi-year mean. The two semantics therefore differ by at most the ≤2% year effect on the mean (plus whatever direction/shape atypicality that chosen year carries), while climatology anchoring removes both the selection-noise term and the no-qualifying-year failure mode by construction.

## Q4 — Threshold practice: the 2% gate, the latest-year rule, documented fallbacks

- **The ~2% annual-mean deviation gate has a documented analog:** NB/T 31147-2018 §5.2 (per complete-text copies, see Q1) — correction to representative-year data is triggered when the annual mean wind speed deviation exceeds 2%. No numeric representativeness threshold appears in MEASNET V3, IEC 61400-15-1, FGW TR6, or the NREL/IEA primer — these instead make the *overlap* seasonally complete (≥12 complete months / ≥1 year) and push residual year effects into the uncertainty budget ("Historic wind resource", "Project lifetime variability" categories; NREL Table 11).
- **The latest-qualifying-year tiebreak is CleanMast-local:** no surveyed standard, tool, or paper prescribes picking the most recent qualifying year when several pass. TMY/WRY selection is by statistics (Finkelstein–Schafer-type weighting; similarity heuristics in Lázaro et al. 2025), not recency. GB/T 18710 selects no year at all. [INFERENCE] The latest-year preference is a reasonable freshness heuristic (closest to current climate/measurement conditions) but is undocumented.
- **Documented fallbacks when data don't qualify:** none of the standards silently substitutes the nearest thing. GB/T 18710's method cannot fail on year selection (it anchors at the multi-year mean); its failure mode is "no suitable long-term station" (§5.3.2 preconditions). MEASNET handles unmet requirements through its deviation mechanism (§5 + §8.1): deviations are described and carried as substitute values / additional uncertainty (its integrity Classes D–E are the model: keep the data, add uncertainty, disclose). FGW TR6: a SOLL deviation must be justified, documented, and "zwingend in der Unsicherheiten-Betrachtung berücksichtigt werden"; a MUSS breach makes the report non-conforming. NREL/IEA: no standard, IEs' own documented approaches with uncertainty budgeting.

## Verdict

**(a) Standard practice where documented.** International standards and every surveyed tool (Windographer, WindPRO, WAsP/windkit, DNV WindFarmer, OpenOA, brightwind) target the **long-term (climatology) wind conditions over a ≥10-year reference**: either a synthesized/predicted long-term series or a mean-rescaled measured series. Actual-year selection as a *target* is documented nowhere. The Chinese GB/T 18710-2002 lineage is closer to CleanMast's algebra than any western standard — a 16-sector additive delta on the mast series — but it too anchors the delta at the **multi-year mean** (Appendix A); its 代表年 label means "representative hourly dataset reflecting the long-term average", not a selected calendar year. NB/T 31147-2018 contributes the one documented numeric gate: correct when annual-mean deviation exceeds 2%.

**(b) Evidence weight.** Climatology-mean anchoring: strong indirect support (all standards/tools; MEASNET's wind-index caveat shows the single-year substrate, not the anchor choice, is the structure risk; inter-annual variability of 4–10%/6–30% quantifies the year-noise an actual-year anchor accepts, bounded to ≤2% in the mean by the gate). Actual-year anchoring: no direct literature validation found; its unique benefit (physically coherent real-year temporal/sectoral structure) is preserved equally by climatology anchoring *because both semantics keep the mast year as the substrate* — the actual-year pick only perturbs the target mean by the (≤2%) year effect. Since the two semantics converge in form, the evidence favors anchoring the mean at the climatology, exactly the quantity every other actor in the field targets. No study shows the choice measurably moves AEP beyond the ~2%-of-mean scale (≈6×-weighted in energy via $E[u^3]$), so the difference is real but small and bounded.

**(c) v1 design implications (ticket 12 Q3).** Keep the additive per-sector delta × slope correction (GB/T 18710 Appendix A is additive-only; the slope multiplication is a CleanMast refinement consistent with sector-regression MCP practice). Change the anchor from the selected representative year's meso mean to the **climatology (complete-years) meso mean** — this matches GB/T 18710/NB/T (the 行标 fidelity target), MEASNET/IEC expectations, and tool behavior; it also removes the selection-noise term and the failure mode. If exact 行标 compatibility is required, the global-offset variant (ticket 12's description) is the closest match, with the sectorized fit binned by mast direction per 18710. Recommended framing: make climatology anchoring the default, retain actual-year anchoring as an explicit compatibility option, and keep the 2% need-check as a **reporting/uncertainty flag** ("mast year deviates >2% from climatology → add uncertainty / disclose"), mirroring NB/T 31147's trigger semantics rather than a hard gate.

**(d) No-qualifying-year failure mode.** With climatology anchoring the failure mode disappears by construction (GB/T 18710's method has no year-selection step to fail). If actual-year mode is retained, the **hard error is the defensible default**: every surveyed standard handles unmet requirements via documented deviation + uncertainty adder (MEASNET substitute uncertainty; FGW SOLL justification + uncertainty; never silent substitution), and a nearest-year fallback would silently ship a year the documented 2% criterion (NB/T 31147) says is unrepresentative. If a fallback is ever wanted, it must be an explicit, logged deviation with an uncertainty penalty — i.e., behaviorally identical to surfacing the problem, which the hard error already does more honestly.
