# Research: WD ε threshold — direction-sensor accuracy and field-uncertainty evidence (ticket 19)

Date: 2026-09-02. Question: what ε (degrees) should the ε-group sort use when ranking WD candidates? Ticket 07 set ε = 2° for vertical ("smaller gaps are below direction-sensor accuracy ≈ noise"); ticket 15 parked its Q3 (horizontal ε) on this note after the user flagged that common mast WD sensors carry a documented 误差许可 that should ground the choice. The ε semantic: candidates whose wrapped-MAE gap is < ε are grouped as ties and the secondary metric (circular-mean bias) breaks the tie. ε too small → noise ranks as signal; ε too large → real skill differences collapse into ties.

**Chinese 规范 instrumentation requirements (the 误差许可 the user referenced):**

## Q2 — Total field direction-uncertainty budget: sensor spec + mounting/alignment

**The wind-tunnel-calibrated sensor is sub-degree; the field total is several degrees, and the alignment/boom term — not the sensor — dominates.**

- **Calibration-lab floor.** Deutsche WindGuard's WD-sensor calibration report (procedure per IEC 61400-12-1:2017 Annex N): tunnel centerline determined to 0.1°, flow-direction parallelism checked to 0.1°, rotary encoder calibration uncertainty 0.3°, two opposite yaw sweeps to ≥380° for hysteresis, evaluation by 5° bins with vector averaging per ISO 16622/VDI 3786. "The total uncertainty is calculated to be 0.4 degree", stated as expanded uncertainty k=2 in certificates with a DAkkS-agreed minimum of 0.8° (k=2). https://www.windguard.de/veroeffentlichungen.html?file=files%2Fcto_layout%2Fimg%2Funternehmen%2Fveroeffentlichungen%2F2018%2FCalibration+of+wind+direction+sensors.pdf . So a lab-calibrated vane is good to ≲1° (k=2) — calibration is not the bottleneck. (Analogous sub-degree numbers u(θ)≈0.4° appear in nacelle-lidar field-calibration practice built on IEC 61400-12-1 Annex E: https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/8/11/907 .)
- **IEC treatment.** IEC 61400-12-1:2017 does not set a numeric acceptance value for direction; it structures the budget: Annex N (informative wind-tunnel calibration procedure for WD sensors, with example uncertainty tables) and Annex E.12.2/E.13 (Category-B uncertainty components for direction from vanes/sonics and their combination). Catalog/preview records: https://standards.iteh.ai/catalog/standards/clc/8655c4e3-8a13-4f9f-89c4-f555cd79dc8a/en-iec-61400-12-1-2022 ; content per the WindGuard report above (read directly). The successor measurement standard IEC 61400-50-1:2022 carries the WD calibration in its Annex A, interpreted for mutual use by MEASNET's "Procedure for wind direction sensor calibration" (May 2025; 0.1° reference-angle positioning tolerance, 8 m/s ±0.8 m/s calibration speed, alignment uncertainty explicitly carried): announcement record https://www.measnet.com/2025/05/new-measnet-procedure-wind-direction-sensor-calibration/ (PDF returned 404 on all fetch attempts this session — flagged).
- **MEASNET site-assessment guidance (V3, 2022).** §7.3: direction "shall be performed according to IEC 61400-12-1 … using high quality devices that should be calibrated according to IEC 61400-12-1 Ed.2 by a calibration laboratory accredited according to ISO 17025"; vanes on separate booms per 12-1 orientation/length rules; "Accurate alignment of the wind vane shall be performed during its installation in order to allow for wind direction offset correction of the data"; at least one additional vane ≥20 m lower for veer/availability. §8.6 Table 4 makes direction an explicit uncertainty topic with components "Direction sensor characteristics (… orientation, in-situ comparison)" and "Quality of correction method applied"; §11.2 reporting requires "description of the orientation of the north mark of the measurement devices in geographic north and documentation of the alignment procedure applied". No numeric tolerance anywhere — alignment is mandated, documented, and carried as uncertainty. https://measnet.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/ce599b8a-639f-4b48-992e-a099e1b14956.pdf .
- **Field north-alignment error — quantified.** The problem is real enough to sustain a product category: the Kintech **Geovane** (solar-azimuth true-north compass pairing with Thies vanes) sells "wind vane orientation with uncertainty <1°" (independent IEC/ISO 17025 validation; Thies+Geovane bundles claim true-north direction "within 1°") — https://www.kintech-engineering.com/pdf_docs/EN_Geovane_User_Guide.pdf ; https://www.geovane360.com/ ; https://www.thiesclima.com/en/home/say-goodbye-to-inaccurate-wind-data-try-first-class-amp-geovane-today/ . Thies' own installation manual concedes the classical method's fragility: align by compass toward a landmark, "please consider the local declination … and local magnetic interferences (e.g. hardware, electric cable)" (4.3151 manual §5.2). The one peer-reviewed field measurement found: **Lee (2008), Sensors 8:7783** — a carefully installed 46 m mast (ground-aligned, then erected) at Daegwanryung showed a measured vane misalignment of **16.14° ± 2.13° (std, n=36) from true north**, attributed to erection twist and mast torsion; the paper explicitly decomposes direction error into lab-correctable sensor nonlinearity plus installation discordance, "which can only be minimized by aligning … at the time of installation". https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/sensors/sensors-08-07783/article_deploy/sensors-08-07783.pdf?version=1403310410 . NREL/AWS handbook §6.5 likewise devotes a section to compass declination/GPS true-north determination without quantifying attainable accuracy (op. cit.). So several-degrees alignment error is the documented norm and tail cases reach >10°.

**Budget synthesis (legacy fleet, per sensor, expanded ≈ k=2):** calibrated-or-spec sensor ±2.5°–4.3° ⊕ boom/north alignment ~±2°–5° (unquantified by any standard; bounded below by Geovane's <1° best case and above by the 16° documented failure) → **total field direction uncertainty ≈ ±3°–5° typical, unbounded in bad installs**. A modern 200M/First-Class + Geovane mast achieves ≈ ±1.6°–2°. Note the systematic (bias-like) character of the alignment term: it is fixed per mast/sensor and unknown without an audit.

## Q3 — Meaningful-gap evidence: what wrapped-MAE gap between two candidates is statistically/physically meaningful

- **Large samples can statistically resolve sub-degree differences.** Bao, Gneiting, Grimit, Guttorp & Raftery (2010, MWR 138:1811, doi:10.1175/2009MWR3138.1; preprint read in full) evaluated 48-h surface-wind-direction forecasts over the Pacific Northwest with mean circular absolute error (same wrap-to-shortest-arc definition as our wrapped-MAE). Averaged over 54 stations × 365 days × 8 members (~150k cases): raw ensemble 45.14°/42.8°; bias-corrected (circular–circular regression, 28-day training) 42.88°/39.3° — a 2.3–2.8° improvement; and *between post-processing methods* the spread at fixed training length was only 0.3°–0.5° (Table 3: 42.88 vs 43.18 vs 43.36 at 28 days), yet was interpreted as method ranking. Two further anchors: (i) they verify "only when the verifying wind speed was at least 5 kt (2.57 m/s), since wind direction observations are unreliable at lower wind speeds" — independent corroboration of ticket 07's ws ≥ 3 m/s exclusion; (ii) their ~40° raw model-vs-observation error is a magnitude reference for how coarse even 48-h NWP direction is. https://sites.stat.washington.edu/raftery/Research/PDF/Bao2010MWR.pdf .
- **MCP benchmarking treats direction as its own track with distribution-level scores, not sub-degree ranks.** Rogers, Rogers & Manwell (2005, JWIA 93(3):243–264, doi:10.1016/j.jweia.2004.12.002) scored each MCP algorithm on six aspects including the wind-direction distribution (record-level access paywalled; abstract: https://www.osti.gov/etdeweb/biblio/20648557 ; as compiled in `research/mcp-benchmarking.md`). No MCP study located reports *time-series* wrapped-MAE differences between direction methods at sub-2° resolution with significance testing; Jonietz Alvarez et al. 2024 use "mean wind-direction difference" as a distribution statistic (per `mcp-benchmarking.md`).
- **Meso-vs-mast direction error is ~10°–20°+.** EMD validates its EMD-WRF EUROPE+ (ERA5-driven) mesoscale dataset against ~300 masts using WD MAE (periodicity-resolved) as a headline metric, EMD-EUR+ reducing WD MAE ~10% vs ERA5 (https://help.emd.dk/knowledgebase/content/TechNotes/TechnicalNote7_EMDWRF_EUROPE_Validation.pdf); ERA5 offshore directional offsets of ~10°–20° (https://www.mdpi.com/2077-1312/13/1/149); ERA5 complex-terrain median angular errors 16°–80° by diurnal cluster (https://doi.org/10.5194/egusphere-egu26-12857). So at horizontal scale the *absolute* candidate MAE level is ~10–20° and method differences are plausibly a few degrees — an ε of 2° would purport to rank candidates at a precision finer than the truth sensor's own field uncertainty.
- **Fold noise is of the same order as 2°.** Veer (the vertical signal) is stability/season-dependent — regime-dependent magnitudes ~25–45° across the ABL, up to ~43° in the first 100 m on stable nights (Abkar & Porté-Agel 2014, https://doi.org/10.1088/1742-6596/524/1/012118), with quasi-universal stability relations across sites (Kelly & van der Laan 2023, WES 8:975, https://doi.org/10.5194/wes-8-975-2023) — so month-scale vs series-scale fitted offsets genuinely differ across monthly folds by ~1–3°, and a 2° MAE gap will re-rank with fold composition. The ε-group sort exists precisely to absorb this.
- **Consumption granularity is 22.5°–30°.** GB/T 18709/18710 consume direction as a 16-sector × 22.5° rose (§4.2.2/§5.4.4); MEASNET V3 §11 reporting uses ≤30° sectors (first centred on geographic north, op. cit.); wake/energy-yield sensitivity analyses are run per direction sector. No downstream consumer of the reconstructed series distinguishes a 2° direction improvement — but 5°+ begins to matter for sector-boundary assignment and veer-bias accumulation.

## Q4 — Cross-scope consequence: should horizontal and vertical ε differ, and is ticket 07's 2° wrong?

One honest subtlety first: **the sensor-spec argument is a meaningfulness floor, not a hard statistical one.** All CleanMast WD candidates are offset-type predictors (θ̂ = θ_source + Δ per conditioning); a fixed truth-sensor error δ (alignment) shifts every candidate's error by the same δ, so the *paired MAE gap* is largely invariant to δ — with a year of 10-min data the exam can statistically separate candidates well below sensor accuracy (Q3, Bao et al.). The reason ε must still sit at the field-uncertainty level is that a gap below the truth reference's documented uncertainty (a) cannot be transferred as a claim — the "winner's" production series inherits ≥ that error against any absolute north reference, (b) is smaller than fold-composition noise (Q3), and (c) is invisible to every downstream consumer (Q3, 22.5°–30° sectors).

Given that: **no, there is no defensible reason for horizontal and vertical ε to differ.** Both exams score candidates against a vane-measured truth on the target mast; the irreducible truth-reference term (sensor spec ⊕ that boom's north-mark alignment, per Q1/Q2 ≈ ±3°–5° legacy, ≈ ±2° modern) is identical in structure and magnitude in both scopes. The horizontal exam's larger source errors (meso ~10–20°, Q3) inflate the *absolute* MAE level and the *between-candidate* spread, not the tie floor; the vertical exam's smaller signal (adjacent-layer veer of a few degrees) makes the same floor *more* binding, not less. Ticket 07's ε = 2° was set by "direction-sensor accuracy ≈ noise", which (i) used a best-case modern sensor spec (±1°–2°) rather than the legacy-fleet budget (±2.5°–5°), and (ii) ignored the alignment term that every surveyed standard carries as an explicit, separately documented uncertainty component. Ticket 15 Q2's framing ("source–target differences dwarf sensor noise") concerns the *signal*, and is why ε must not be raised so far that real horizontal method differences collapse; it does not license a smaller ε than the truth-reference floor.

## Verdict

**(a) Documented sensor 误差许可.** NRG 200P ±4.3° (k=2; datasheet "linearity within 1%" + 4°–8° dead band) and its 200M successor ±1.6°–2.5° (k=2, dead-band-free); Thies First Class 4.3151 ±1° (0.1° resolution) — the ticket's "4.3351" is Thies' anemometer, the vane is 4.3151; Vector W200P ±3° (>5 m/s steady winds; ±2° after calibration, repeatability ±0.5°) — "A100" is Vector's cup anemometer, the vane family is W200P/W100P; Met One 024A ±5°; Vaisala WAV151 better than ±3° with a 5.6° quantization step (WMT52 sonic ±3°). Chinese 规范: GB/T 18709-2002 §5.1.2.2 **风向传感器 精确度 ±2.5°** (with §6.2.2.3 mandating magnetic-declination-corrected true-north installation); GB/T 18710-2002 contains no direction-accuracy clause (defers to 18709; only the 22.5° inter-height plausibility bound); NB/T 31147-2018 tightens procurement to **≤±1° error, ≤0.5° resolution, dead-band-free preferred** (per complete-text copies — paywalled official text, flagged). US lineage: NREL/AWS handbook Table 5.1 wind-vane system error **≤5°**.

**(b) Total field direction-uncertainty budget.** Sensor (±2.5°–4.3° legacy; ±1°–1.6° modern) ⊕ north/boom alignment. Calibration is not the bottleneck — wind-tunnel certificates reach 0.4° (k=1) / ≥0.8° (k=2) (WindGuard, IEC 61400-12-1 Annex N / 61400-50-1 Annex A via MEASNET 2025) — alignment is: mandated and documented but never quantified by MEASNET V3 (§7.3, Table 4, §11.2), best-case <1° with a Geovane solar compass, typical compass practice unquantified, and measured at **16.14° ± 2.13°** on a well-installed 46 m mast (Lee 2008). **Legacy-fleet total ≈ ±3°–5° (expanded, bias-like, per-sensor-fixed); modern instrumented masts ≈ ±2°.**

**(c) Recommended ε: 5°, for both scopes.** Justification: 5° equals the loosest documented system-error spec among the legacy fleet (NREL/AWS ≤5°; Met One 024A ±5°), envelopes NRG 200P's ±4.3° (k=2) and GB/T 18709's ±2.5° sensor term ⊕ a conservative alignment term (RSS ≈ 3.2°–4.7°), sits safely above monthly-fold gap noise (~0.5°–3°; veer seasonality per Kelly & van der Laan 2023) so genuinely different candidate families (global vs per-sector, month vs series scale) still separate, and stays an order of magnitude below the 22.5°–30° sector granularity at which direction is actually consumed. A documented-modern-instrumentation clause is defensible: only where the mast's record shows a ±1°-class sensor (Thies 4.3151 / NRG 200M / NB/T 31147-compliant) **and** sub-1° north referencing (Geovane-class, per MEASNET §11.2 documentation) may ε drop to 2°; absent that audit trail, 5° is the honest floor.

**(d) Ticket 07's vertical ε = 2° should be amended to the same 5°** (with the same modern-instrumentation exception). The vertical exam's truth reference is a same-mast vane carrying the identical sensor-spec + boom-alignment uncertainty (per-boom, hence not common to the anchor vane below), so the tie floor is the same as horizontal; 2° encoded a best-case sensor spec and ignored the alignment term that every surveyed standard treats as a first-class uncertainty component. Uniform ε also keeps the ε-group semantics and benchmark artifacts comparable across `benchmark-vertical` (tickets 07/08) and `benchmark-horizontal` (tickets 06/15). Feeds ticket 15 Q3 (horizontal ε) and amends ticket 07 decision 9.
